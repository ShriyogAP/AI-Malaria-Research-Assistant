# Curated Malaria Research Knowledge Base

This is an educational collection, not a complete clinical reference.

## SOURCE: World Health Organization — Malaria Fact Sheet

URL: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria

Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads mainly through bites from infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Common symptoms include fever, chills, and headache. Severe symptoms can include confusion, seizures, and difficulty breathing. Diagnosis requires parasite-based testing, such as microscopy or a rapid diagnostic test. Prevention includes reducing mosquito bites.

## SOURCE: CDC — About Malaria

URL: https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/about/index.html

Malaria is caused by parasites and most often spreads through bites from infected Anopheles mosquitoes. Symptoms include fever, chills, headache, muscle aches, tiredness, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. A healthcare provider diagnoses malaria using laboratory testing. Prevention includes avoiding mosquito bites and consulting a healthcare professional about preventive medicines when appropriate.

## SOURCE: CDC — Testing for Malaria

URL: https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/testing/

A blood sample is needed to confirm malaria. Blood-smear microscopy can detect parasites and help identify the species. Rapid diagnostic tests can provide useful information but have limitations. People with possible malaria exposure and symptoms should seek prompt medical assessment.

## SOURCE: CDC — Symptoms of Malaria

URL: https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/symptoms/

Malaria symptoms may include fever, chills, headache, muscle aches, tiredness, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. Severe disease can cause anemia, jaundice, kidney failure, seizures, confusion, coma, or death. Symptoms alone cannot confirm malaria. People with possible exposure and compatible symptoms should seek medical care promptly.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can learn visual patterns from labeled blood-cell images to classify infected and uninfected cells.
